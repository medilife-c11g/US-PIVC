# Response to Reviewers — The Ultrasound Journal (Submission #19152)

**Manuscript:** Post-insertion outcomes of ultrasound-guided versus landmark peripheral intravenous catheters: a systematic review and meta-analysis
**Decision:** Revisions Required (Prof. Francesco Corradi)

We thank the Editor and both reviewers for their constructive and encouraging assessment. We are especially grateful for the recognition that the manuscript addresses a clinically relevant gap with a balanced, methodologically sound synthesis. We have revised the manuscript to address every point. All changes are shown in **red** in the marked-up file; a clean version is also provided. Reviewer comments are reproduced below in *italics*, each followed by our response and the location of the change.

---

## Reviewer 1 (Recommendation: Accept)

> *The statement "We hypothesised that USG insertion would not confer a significant advantage…" may be perceived as reflecting an a priori expectation… The authors may wish to rephrase this sentence to emphasize the objective of evaluating differences between techniques rather than stating an expectation of no benefit.*

**Response.** We agree and have removed the a-priori-expectation framing. The closing sentence of the Introduction now reads:

> "Rather than presuming a direction of effect, we sought to evaluate whether post-insertion catheter performance differs between ultrasound-guided and landmark placement."

*(Introduction, final paragraph.)* We thank Reviewer 1 for the recommendation to accept.

## Reviewer 2 (Recommendation: Revisions Required)

**Major comments**

> *1. In the Abstract "Results"… "Fourteen studies … were included" should be modified as: "… were included in qualitative synthesis."*

**Response.** Changed as requested, and updated to reflect the newly included Avelar 2015 trial (major point 5). The Abstract Results now reads "**Fifteen** studies (**6** randomized trials, 9 cohort studies; 78,591 participants) were included **in the qualitative synthesis**." *(Abstract.)*

> *2. Why were Scopus and Web of Science databases excluded? This point should be addressed in the study limitations.*

**Response.** We searched four databases (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, CINAHL) and did not search Scopus or Web of Science. We have added this explicitly to the new Study Limitations section: "We searched four major databases but did not include Scopus or Web of Science; although we cross-referenced three prior systematic reviews and hand-searched the reference lists of included studies (identifying no additional eligible records), records uniquely indexed in Scopus or Web of Science may have been missed." *(Study Limitations, item 1.)*

> *3. In "Study Selection"… "19 records advanced to full-text retrieval." Did the authors do any further checking?*

**Response.** Yes. Beyond the database search, we (i) hand-searched the reference lists of all included studies and of three prior USG-PIVC systematic reviews to identify additional eligible records, and (ii) retrieved two records (Desai 2018; Nishizawa 2020) that were not initially available via institutional access through interlibrary loan. We have made this "further checking" explicit in Study Selection. *(Methods — Study Selection.)*

> *4. … unclear if [full-text screening] was performed again by the two independent reviewers or by the authors (all or part of them).*

**Response.** We have clarified the full process. Title/abstract screening was performed independently by two reviewers (C.C.C. and Y.L.W.) in Rayyan, with the inter-rater statistics reported. **Full-text eligibility assessment was then performed independently by two reviewers (T.A.L. and Y.L.W.), with the senior author (C.C.C.) adjudicating; final inclusion decisions were reached by consensus discussion among all three.** We also note that the second screening reviewer is author Y.L.W. (Yu-Ling Wang); the initials have been standardized to "Y.L.W." throughout (the submitted Methods had abbreviated this reviewer inconsistently). The Author Contributions statement has been corrected to match this process. *(Methods — Study Selection; Author Contributions.)*

> *5. "Two Avelar et al. reports, Portuguese-language full text not available…" — but the search was restricted to English-language publications. This is confusing. Please clarify.*

**Response.** We thank the reviewer — this prompted us to re-examine the two Avelar records, and the reviewer is correct that the original handling was inconsistent. On re-checking we found that the two records are **two reports of the same trial** (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00930254): a 2013 report in *Revista da Escola de Enfermagem da USP* that is genuinely **in Portuguese** (English abstract only), and a 2015 report in the *Journal of Infusion Nursing* that is **in English**. The English 2015 report is an RCT comparing ultrasound-guided versus landmark PIVC insertion in children and reports post-insertion outcomes (infiltration and phlebitis); it therefore meets our eligibility criteria. **We have now included the Avelar trial via its English 2015 report** (Avelar AF, et al. *J Infus Nurs.* 2015;38[5]:320–327) and cite the 2013 Portuguese article as a companion report of the same trial, excluded only as a non-English duplicate report (handled identically to the multiple Kleidon/EPIC reports). This raises the included set to **15 studies (6 RCTs, 9 cohort studies)** and adds the trial's infiltration data to the meta-analysis. Notably, Avelar used the **same catheter length (19 mm) in both arms** (an unconfounded comparison) and found infiltration **significantly higher with USG** (34/161 vs 23/178; P=.025). The updated infiltration meta-analysis (now k=3) gives a pooled RR of **1.30 (95% CI 0.82–2.05; I²=48.8%)**, consistent with the review's overall conclusion of no post-insertion benefit of USG. We also note in the manuscript a differential-ascertainment caveat for this trial (complications in the USG arm were assessed with ultrasound visualization, potentially inflating detected infiltration). *(Methods — Study Selection; Results — Infiltration; PRISMA flow; Tables 1–2; Risk of Bias.)*

> *6. … include an autonomous "Study limitations" section (before "Clinical Implications"), described in more detail as a numbered list. The main limitations should be reported also in the Abstract conclusions.*

**Response.** Done. We have created a standalone, numbered **Study Limitations** section immediately before Clinical Implications (relocating and expanding the limitations previously embedded in the Discussion). The Abstract Conclusions now also carries a brief limitations statement: "These findings are constrained by a small, heterogeneous evidence base, catheter-specification confounding in several positive studies, and low-to-very-low GRADE certainty." *(New Study Limitations section; Abstract Conclusions.)*

> *7. In all Tables and Figures the enrolled studies should be reordered chronologically, starting from the most recent article. Following these criteria also in the text, especially for the "Results" section.*

**Response.** Done. The study tables (Table 1, and Table 2 within each of its RoB-2 and Newcastle–Ottawa sections) and all forest plots (Figures 3–5 and the relocated Supplementary Figures 1–2) have been reordered most-recent-first, and the Results narrative follows the same order. The meta-analytic figures were regenerated in R with chronological study ordering; pooled estimates are unchanged (the reordering affects display order only). *(Tables 1–2; Figures 3–5; Results.)*

> *8. Figures 5–6 should be relocated to the supplementary material, by reducing the text of the "Time-to-event-adjacent estimates (not pooled)" section.*

**Response.** Done. The extravasation forest plot and the time-to-event-adjacent plot (the reviewer's Figures 5 and 6) have been relocated to the Supplementary Material as Supplementary Figures 1 and 2, respectively. The main figures are now Figure 1 (risk-of-bias summary), Figure 2 (PRISMA flow diagram), Figure 3 (catheter failure), Figure 4 (dwell time), Figure 5 (infiltration), and Figure 6 (catheter failure by age subgroup); the infiltration forest plot was retained as a main figure. The "Time-to-event-adjacent estimates (not pooled)" subsection has been condensed accordingly, retaining only the essential interpretation. *(Results; Supplementary Material.)*

> *9. Reduce the length of "Studies with Major Confounding." Report bibliographical references uniformly through Results and Discussion {e.g., Dachepally et al. (2023)[Ref. No.] …}.*

**Response.** Done. The "Studies with Major Confounding" subsection has been shortened (study-level detail condensed; full data remain in Table 1). Throughout the Results and Discussion, author–year mentions are now uniformly followed by the bracketed reference number (e.g., "Dachepally et al. (2023) [22]", "Paladini et al. (2018) [23]"). *(Results; Discussion.)*

**Response.** We apologise — the supplementary figure file was inadvertently omitted from the original upload. The supplementary figures (the extravasation and time-to-event forest plots, now S1 and S2) are included with this resubmission, and the four in-text callouts have been reconciled to the corrected supplementary numbering. *(Supplementary Material.)*
